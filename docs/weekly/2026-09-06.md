# Week 18: Two Node Failures in One Week

*Covers roughly 31 August - 6 September 2026.*

This week was spent scratching my head. Two nodes failed, deus and opus, in completely
different ways, and neither left much evidence behind. Both now have postmortems.

## 1. deus locks up and takes remote access with it

On 2 September, deus simply stopped. No shutdown, no kernel panic, no out of memory
kill, nothing in the logs. It was idle at the time.

The cluster itself was fine. etcd kept quorum on sol and opus, and the public site
kept serving through the Cloudflare tunnel. But deus is the **only Tailscale subnet
router**, so losing it removed every way of reaching the cluster from outside the
house: SSH, `kubectl`, ArgoCD, Grafana and every other `*.lab` service. From my point
of view it looked like everything was down.

A physical reboot fixed it. Digging through the journal afterwards showed this was the
third silent lockup in four months, all with the same signature.

A second problem turned up while fixing the first. I installed Tailscale on sol and
opus to give them their own tailnet identities, and both came up accepting deus's
routes. They started sending their own LAN traffic, including etcd traffic, through
the tunnel to deus and back, and nodes flapped between Ready and NotReady until I
turned `--accept-routes` off.

Full write-up: [deus silent lockup and total tailnet plane outage](../postmortems/2026-09-02-deus-lockup.md).

## 2. opus and the reset loop

opus was much more frustrating. It first died outright with blinking red lights. After
bringing it back, it started hard resetting roughly every 30 minutes to an hour, which
showed up as a row of gaps in the Grafana dashboards.

I suspected a failing CMOS battery, high temperatures and several other things. The
journal did not support any of them: every reset happened in the middle of perfectly
ordinary activity, with no reason given. I drained opus and stopped K3s completely,
and it still reset while sitting idle. That at least ruled out anything in the
cluster.

The fix came from other people running this same model as a home server. A search
through Grok surfaced reports that the Intel integrated graphics can reset these
machines when switching display power states. I added `i915.enable_dc=0` to the GRUB
kernel command line and, at the same time, replaced the CMOS battery. Since then opus
has stayed up with K3s scheduling re-enabled.

Because I changed two things at once (and possibly reseated the memory while swapping
the battery), I cannot say for certain which one fixed it. The postmortem is written
with that caveat, and I will only call it resolved once opus survives a full week.

Full write-up: [opus reset loop](../postmortems/2026-09-06-opus-reset-loop.md).
